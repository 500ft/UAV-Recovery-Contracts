R1 — HIGH: RECEIVE-TIME NEIGHBORS ARE NOT AUTOMATIC EVENT-TIME BOUNDS

Location: protocols/event-semantics.md section 7; inherited implementation in
harness/trace.py bracket(), host_instant(), vehicle_instant(); D16.

The assertion that two monotonically advancing clocks guarantee containment
between neighboring received telemetry timestamps is false without a transport
or sampling assumption. For example, in a simple common-time illustration:
  sample generated at 8 arrives at host time 10;
  local injection occurs at host time 11;
  sample generated at 9 arrives at host time 12.
Both clocks and the two received samples are monotone, yet the apparent [8,9]
interval does not contain the event at 11. Delayed telemetry causes the error.
For a delayed HEARTBEAT or STATUSTEXT, its own generation time is another unknown.

The runner's v.boot_s is also the timestamp of the most recently pumped
telemetry message. Recording that value while stopping a stream preserves a
real vehicle observation; it does not establish that the stream stopped at
that exact vehicle instant. Currently vehicle_instant makes it a point interval.

Minimum correction today:
- Preserve sample time, host receive time and local action time as different
  quantities. Name cached telemetry observations as such and retain their age.
- Native uLog event timestamps remain useful event observations, subject to
  timestamp resolution and event-definition semantics.
- Mark heuristic reconstructed bounds as uncalibrated. Without a justified
  finite bound, use an open interval and an inconclusive timing comparison.
- A finite upper bound needs a defensible latency/timebase bound or a causally
  post-event sample/acknowledgment with specified timestamp semantics. Merely
  receiving the next queued packet does not supply that.
- Do not clamp a point estimate into an unjustified interval and call it known.

Acceptance fixture: the delayed-telemetry example above cannot yield an exact
injection time or a confident timing verdict. Historical qualitative absence
of recovery may remain reportable; recompute timing conclusions separately.

R3 — HIGH FOR INTERPRETATION: REPEATABILITY IS NOT MEASUREMENT ACCURACY

Location: protocols/expected-timelines.json tolerance basis; D20/D21;
harness/reproducibility.py names and docstring; evidence README.

The 0.352-second value is the observed range of a particular event's boot-relative
timestamps across four valid control repeats. It can contain startup, execution,
sampling and detection variability. It is not by itself an upper bound on the
error of an injection-relative latency. Four repeats do not establish a tail
bound, and clock provenance alone does not isolate apparatus error.

Recommendation:
- KEEP 1.5 seconds for re-analysis under the historical development criterion.
- Call 0.352 seconds the observed repeat-to-repeat timestamp range; report the
  four-run sample size, event anchor and observation route.
- Remove 'the safe direction' and '4.3 times real jitter' as validation claims.
  A wider tolerance reduces false mismatch alarms but can hide real deviations.
- Do not replace the number with 1.1 seconds just by multiplying this range.
- For FUTURE confirmation, a new protocol version may use separate development
  calibration to choose a defensible tolerance. Freeze it before new held-out
  runs and keep historical reports under their original criterion.
- Keep the development 1.5-second criterion distinct from Study A's proposed
  median 0.2-second/p95 1.0-second criteria; supersede or retain them explicitly.

This fixes the reasoning without rewriting historical results to look better.
